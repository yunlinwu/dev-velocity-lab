I've started and quit meditation more times than I can count, mostly in my twenties. This time it stuck, and the reason is a shift in what I think it's for.

I just finished Joe Dispenza's *Breaking the Habit of Being Yourself*. His definition of meditation is simple: **self-observation**. Not emptying your mind, not feeling calm. Just watching yourself closely enough to notice what you're doing on autopilot.

That reframe changed how I use it.

## We're hamsters on a wheel

Most of what I do all day, I don't choose. I run the same thinking, the same reactions, the same habits, and I don't notice until much later. The problem isn't that these habits are bad in isolation. It's that I can't see them while they're happening, so I can't change them.

A few minutes of quiet creates a gap. In that gap I can ask: what am I doing right now, and why? Am I reacting to this, or choosing it?

## The pattern I actually found

When work gets hard, I do the minimum. I look for any excuse to get comfortable: take a break, grab comfort food, zone out. Underneath that is fear, of failing and of what people will think.

This isn't just a work thing. It shows up in my personal life and in my relationships too. Whenever I'm stretched, my instinct is to get away from the difficulty.

The second pattern is anger. When something doesn't go my way, or an inconvenience means extra work, I get irritated. I don't say anything. I just complain silently, and then I pull away from people. I end up tense and closed off, which is exactly the opposite of who I want to be.

I didn't see either pattern clearly until I started looking for gaps.

## What's different now

I used to know, in the moment, that I was going to run. Now I'm starting to catch it sooner. The next time something hard shows up, the goal isn't to force myself to be fearless. It's to notice the urge to escape and choose to stay with the problem for a bit longer.

The same goes for anger. I'm trying to be more forgiving in the moment, complain less, and let it go instead of stewing.

I'm also trying not to turn this into another task on my list. My old habit was to treat self-improvement like a checkbox: "Ten minutes of meditation, daily, no excuses." That tends to fall apart the first time I miss a day. So I just do it when I can, and I focus on the gaps rather than the streak.

## What I'd take from this

- **Notice the escape hatch.** Next time you feel the pull to skip, take a break, or go quiet on a hard problem, name it: "I'm running right now." That alone breaks the autopilot.
- **Write down your two or three repeating patterns.** Mine are avoiding hard work and silent anger. You probably have your own. You can't change what you haven't named.
- **Use short gaps, not long sessions.** A few quiet minutes are enough to step off the wheel for a moment.
- **Don't make it a streak.** Do it when you can. Consistency matters, but a rigid rule is how most of us quit.

I finished the book only last week, so I'm early on this. But for the first time, I can see the loop while I'm in it, and that's the part I'd been missing.
