I had two years of experience, a junior college CS background, and zero confidence at my new enterprise job. I was the person who wouldn't touch code because I might break something. Who wouldn't speak in meetings. Who compared myself to every creator online and thought I had nothing to offer.

Then I decided to bet on myself and start YouTube.

Not because I had a grand strategy. Not because I knew what I was doing. But because I was tired of watching other people just *do it* while I stayed stuck.

## The Confidence Came From Reps, Not Talent

Here's what actually happened: by my 10th YouTube video, I wasn't scared anymore.

By the 10th upload—the thumbnails, the settings, the microphone setup, the editing—it became muscle memory. Natural. The fear didn't disappear because I got better at YouTube. It disappeared because I did the same thing repeatedly and nothing bad happened.

Then I realized: this is exactly how I needed to approach work.

I started committing code imperfectly. Shipping fixes without overthinking them. Taking action even when I wasn't sure. And the same pattern held: action killed the fear, not confidence.

## The Real Block Wasn't Skill—It Was Self-Judgment

I wasn't afraid of breaking the application. I was afraid of being judged. Of not being good enough. Of someone seeing my work and thinking I didn't belong there.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: everyone is thinking the exact same thing about *themselves*. They're not watching you closely enough to judge you. They're too busy worrying about how *you're* judging *them*.

Once I stopped assuming I was the center of everyone's attention, I could actually move.

## What Changed at Work

- I stopped waiting for perfection before shipping
- I started speaking up in meetings instead of staying quiet
- I became comfortable hosting or directing meetings—something that used to terrify me
- I picked up harder coding problems instead of waiting until I felt "ready"

The YouTube channel didn't teach me these skills. It taught me the mental model: commit imperfectly, iterate, compare yourself only to where you were last week, ignore the comparison trap.

## The Mindset That Actually Works

Don't wait to feel good enough. Take the smallest action this week. Then a slightly bigger one next week. Stop tuning into what others are doing and tune into what *you* did two weeks ago versus today.

Growth doesn't happen overnight. It happens through repetition, through shipping things that scare you, and through quieting down the voice that says you're not ready yet.

You are ready. You're just afraid. And the only way through that is to do it anyway.
