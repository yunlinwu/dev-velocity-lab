I was stuck and didn't even realize it.

I'd wake up dreading the day. I'd show up to work running on fumes. I'd tell myself I wasn't the type of person who thrives under pressure, who pushes through discomfort, who stays consistent. And every time something hard came up—a new codebase, a difficult conversation, a learning curve—that story would play on repeat in my head, and I'd back down.

Then I started testing three shifts. They sound simple. They're not magic. But they actually worked.

## Shift 1: Link Discomfort to Pleasure, Avoidance to Pain

I used to think growth and pain were the same thing—and that meant I'd avoid both.

But I started flipping the association. When my alarm went off at 6 AM and I wanted to stay in bed, I'd think: *If I stay here, I'm rushing. I'm late. I'm not ready. That's pain.* But if I get up now, I'm in control. I'm calm. I can actually think. That's pleasure.

Same thing at work. Showing up with energy, connecting with people, taking on challenges that scare me—I started linking those to pleasure, not fear. Not because they feel good in the moment, but because future-me benefits. Discomfort became the signal that I'm moving forward.

This doesn't work every time. But over weeks and months? Subconsciously, I started choosing the hard thing more often than the easy one.

## Shift 2: Rewrite the Story You Tell Yourself

I had a narrative running in my head on loop: *I quit when things get hard. I'm not disciplined. I cave under stress.*

My subconscious mind doesn't know if that's true or false. It just hears it, accepts it, and makes me live it out.

One bad day or one skipped workout doesn't matter. But repeat that story 100 times? It becomes your operating system. Every time something challenging shows up, that voice whispers: *You're not the type who does this.* And you believe it.

So I rewrote it. Instead of "I'm somebody who struggles with discipline," I started telling myself: "I'm somebody who is growing. I'm somebody who chooses consistency over perfection."

It sounds small. It's not. When I catch myself in the old loop, I snap out of it and redirect. The new story isn't aspirational—it's what I'm actually doing, right now, with my actions.

## Shift 3: Build Non-Negotiable Rituals

The story and the mindset don't stick without proof.

I built a daily ritual—could be morning, could be after lunch. Something small and trackable that I do no matter what. Because each time I do it, I'm telling myself: *This is who I am now.* The action reinforces the belief. The belief gives me confidence. The confidence makes me do it again.

The ritual doesn't have to be big. It's the repetition that compounds. Over weeks, you see small progress. That progress is self-belief. Self-belief is everything.

## The Reality Check

None of this happens overnight. I didn't wake up one morning and suddenly feel unstuck.

But over a couple of months? The story changed. The morning wake-up got easier. Work felt different. I started saying yes to things that scared me. And each time I did, the cycle reinforced itself.

Test these shifts for yourself. Try one. See what happens over 30 days. You'll know if it's working because you'll *feel* the difference—not in motivation, but in what you actually do.

That's the only proof that matters.
