I've tried the usual stuff: journaling, motivation videos, ritual routines. They work for a week, maybe two. Then life happens, the fire dies down, and you're back where you started.

Six months ago I started writing my goals twice a day—morning and evening. It sounds stupidly simple. It is. And it's the only system that's actually stuck.

## The real problem with other systems

When I journaled, I'd write stuff down and never go back. I'd lie to myself about consistency. When I listened to motivational talks, they'd fire me up for a bit, but that energy fades fast. You need the next hit, the next video. It becomes performance—you're doing it to feel good, not to actually change.

The rituals become rituals. They stop meaning anything.

## What actually works: the 10x Rule tweak

I took Grant Cardone's 10x Rule exercise—writing big goals each morning and evening—and added my own layer. Instead of just listing what I want to achieve, I also write down:

- Who I want to be
- What I need to improve *right now* (health, sleep, time management, communication—whatever's actually missing)
- What I want to focus on that day

So in the morning, I'm not just writing "ship feature X." I'm writing "I'm someone who communicates clearly with my team" or "I'm someone who manages my energy, not my time." Then I write what that looks like *today*.

At night, I do it again. Same goals often. But the repetition is the point.

## Why twice a day actually matters

Writing it down twice pulls you out of your phone, your Slack, your distractions. Takes two minutes. That's it. But those two minutes reset your focus.

The hard part isn't achieving success—it's *keeping* it. After you level up your results, after you change who you are, what keeps you consistent? What keeps the energy there on day 47 when the novelty's gone?

This does. Because you're not relying on motivation or ritual. You're relying on clarity. You're reminding yourself: *This is still the journey. I'm still pursuing growth.*

## The real test

The system works because it's simple enough that you actually do it. You can't fake it in two sentences. Either you're serious about what you're writing or you're lying to yourself—and you know it.

It keeps me grounded when I'm overwhelmed. It keeps me focused when things are going well. It's not about perfection. Some mornings I write nearly the same thing as yesterday. That's fine. The act is the thing.

I've tried to connect everything I learn—every conference talk, every article, every conversation—into something I can actually *use*. This is one of those things.

If you're feeling stuck, behind, or like you're just spinning your wheels: try it for two weeks. Morning and evening. Five minutes total. See what changes.
