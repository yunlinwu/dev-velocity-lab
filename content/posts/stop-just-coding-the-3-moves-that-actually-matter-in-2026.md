I used to think shipping code was enough. I was wrong.

Last year, I realized I was competing with everyone else exactly like me—same skills, same frameworks, same ability to solve problems. The gap wasn't technical anymore. It was everywhere else.

Here are the three moves that actually move the needle in 2026, because I'm doing them and seeing the results:

## 1. Build a Personal Brand (Even If You're Not Ready)

Everyone can code. Almost nobody can *talk about* what they built in a way that matters.

When I started, I didn't know what an early-career engineer was supposed to share. No breakthrough projects, no unique technical depth. So I stopped waiting to be perfect. I started showing my growth instead—the uncomfortable stuff. Talking on camera. Presenting ideas in meetings. Sharing the soft skills and mindset that actually got me here.

That's the move. Not the finished product. The progression.

Your personal brand doesn't need to be polished or fully planned out. You grow into it. You iterate. But the people who *don't* start? They're invisible in a crowded market. And visibility is ROI.

## 2. Learn and Build With AI Right Now

The bar keeps moving. A few years ago, AI was just a chatbot. Now it's agentic—it does work *for* you. Everything can be automated. And if you're not experimenting with it, you're using yesterday's tools.

Here's what I stopped doing: writing a lot of code myself. Instead, I use AI agents. The second I understand what's needed, I have the agent build it. It works almost perfectly, exactly how I want it.

My entire personal brand workflow runs on this—I spend 1-2 hours a day on everything because I automated it. One agent handles transcripts to titles and descriptions. Another makes thumbnails. Another turns it into LinkedIn posts. I didn't plan the whole pipeline upfront. I just started using AI to solve each step, and learned from actually doing it.

The advantage isn't theoretical. It's real: more time to experiment, more familiarity with new tools, more edge than people still writing boilerplate.

## 3. Join a Community (Because Willpower Burns Out Alone)

I used to do everything myself. No community, no accountability. And what happened? I'd put in intense work for a week, then slip back into old habits. The motivation just died.

Being in a community changes that. People depend on you. You depend on them. Everyone grows together instead of everyone burning out alone.

Building a personal brand from nothing is uncomfortable. Doing something new every day is uncomfortable. But a community holds you accountable in a way you can't hold yourself. You share what's working, iterate together, and stay grounded in long-term growth instead of chasing overnight results.

## The Real Play

This isn't about instant results. I'm not chasing viral moments or quick wins. These three moves are about long-term transformation—building a brand over years, getting comfortable with new tools before they become mandatory, and staying motivated through actual human connection.

Everyone can code in 2026. The engineers who advance are the ones doing these three things. Start now, even if you're not ready. You'll grow into it.
